## washout of wingtips

# Why Do the Trailing Edges of Some Model Airplane Main Wings Curve or Tilt Upwards?

The trailing edge of a model airplane's main wing (especially near the wingtips or aileron areas) tilting or curving upward—or being designed with an upward twist—is known in aerodynamics as **washout** or a specific anti-stall aerodynamic design. 

This design is not merely for aesthetics; it serves several critical safety and flight-control purposes:

---

### 1. Preventing "Tip Stall" (The Core Reason)
* **The Mechanics of Stalling:** When an aircraft's angle of attack becomes too steep (such as during low-speed pulling up, steep climbs, or sharp turns), the wings lose lift, resulting in a stall.
* **The Danger of a Spin:** If the entire wing had a uniform angle of attack, the root of the wing (near the fuselage) would typically stall first. However, if the **wingtips were to stall before the root**, one wing would suddenly lose lift. This causes the aircraft to uncontrollably roll to one side and enter a dangerous spin, which in model aviation usually translates to an immediate crash.
* **How Upward Tilt Helps:** By designing the trailing edge near the wingtip to tilt slightly upward relative to the root, the **angle of attack at the wing root is always greater than at the tip**. As the aircraft approaches its stall threshold, the **center of the wing loses lift and drops slightly first** (giving the pilot a warning to push the stick forward), while the **wingtips maintain healthy airflow and lift**.

### 2. Retaining Aileron Authority During a Stall
* Ailerons are typically located on the outer trailing edges of the main wings.
* If the wingtips stall first, the outer ailerons become completely ineffective, rendering the pilot's control inputs useless.
* The upward-tilted trailing edge/washout design ensures that **even during low-speed, high-angle-of-attack conditions or mild stalls, airflow continues to pass over the wingtips**, preserving roll control so the pilot can recover level flight.

### 3. Enhancing Low-Speed Stability and Forgiving Flight Characteristics
* Many model aircraft (particularly trainers and gliders) need to possess exceptional low-speed flight capabilities and a high margin of error for beginners.
* This aerodynamic layout ensures that the aircraft behaves gently during low-speed landings and windy approaches, refusing to abruptly snap or flip over when pushed near its limits.

---

> **💡 Quick Tip:** 
> If you notice that the **actual aileron control surfaces themselves** are slightly angled upward when the power is off, it may not just be structural washout. Pilots or manuals often configure a **slight upward aileron differential** or neutral reflex to offset roll side-effects caused by dihedral angles or to improve longitudinal/lateral stability.



## breakdown

Here is a breakdown of the key structural components and terminology associated with a fixed-wing's main wing:

### 1. Key Structural Parts of the Main Wing
* **Leading Edge (前缘):** The front edge of the wing that first meets the air as the plane moves forward.
* **Trailing Edge (后缘):** The back edge of the wing where the air leaves. Control surfaces like ailerons and flaps are attached here.
* **Wingtip (翼尖):** The outermost tip of the wing. Often fitted with winglets to reduce drag.
* **Root (翼根):** The inner part of the wing where it attaches to the fuselage.
* **Spar (翼梁):** The main internal structural beam running spanwise through the wing, providing strength to withstand bending loads.

### 2. Control Surfaces on the Main Wing
* **Ailerons (副翼):** Located near the outer trailing edge of the wings. They move in opposite directions (one up, one down) to control the **Roll axis**, banking the aircraft left or right.
* **Flaps (襟翼):** Located closer to the wing root on the trailing edge. They extend downward to increase lift and drag during takeoff and landing.
* **Slats (缝翼):** Located on the leading edge of the wing, extending forward at low speeds to increase lift and prevent stalls.
* **Spoilers / Airbrakes (扰流板/减速板):** Panels on the upper surface of the wing that pop up to decrease lift and increase drag (often used during landing or descent).
