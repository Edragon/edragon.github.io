## wing position 

A **High-Wing** design means the wing is mounted at the top (or toward the top) of the fuselage.

Viewed from the front or rear, the wing-to-fuselage attachment is located at the highest point (or near the top) of the fuselage structure. This layout is very common in general aviation aircraft, military transport planes, and many RC models.

### Main Wing Position Configurations

Based on the vertical mounting position of the wing on the fuselage, besides the High-Wing, the main configurations are as follows:

#### 1. Mid-Wing
- **Definition:** The wing is mounted in the middle of the fuselage (through the center of the fuselage cross-section).
- **Characteristics:** Less aerodynamic interference, balanced lift-to-drag ratio, and good roll performance.
- **Applications:** Common on high-performance fighters (e.g., J-10, F-16), as well as some advanced trainers and aerobatic RC models.

#### 2. Low-Wing
- **Definition:** The wing is mounted at the bottom (or toward the bottom) of the fuselage.
- **Characteristics:** The main landing gear can be retracted directly into the wing (lighter and stronger structure), better ground visibility, and easier passenger boarding.
- **Applications:** Most commercial airliners (e.g., Boeing 737, Airbus A320), as well as many general aviation aircraft and low-wing aerobatic RC models.
