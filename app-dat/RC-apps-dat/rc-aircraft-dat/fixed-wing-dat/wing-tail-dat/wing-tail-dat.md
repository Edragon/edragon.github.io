The tail typically consists of two main aerodynamic surfaces:

### 1. Vertical Stabilizer (Fin & Rudder)
* **Vertical Stabilizer / Fin (垂直安定面/垂尾):** The fixed vertical part that provides directional stability (prevents the tail from swinging side to side).
* **Rudder (方向舵):** The movable control surface attached to the back of the vertical stabilizer, controlling the **Yaw axis** (turning left and right).

### 2. Horizontal Stabilizer (Stabilator & Elevator)
* **Horizontal Stabilizer (水平安定面/平尾):** The fixed horizontal part that provides pitch stability.
* **Elevator (升降舵):** The movable control surface attached to the back of the horizontal stabilizer, controlling the **Pitch axis** (climbing and descending).

---

### Common Tail Configurations & English Terms

Depending on the shape and layout of the tail, there are several common terms used in fixed-wing aircraft and drones:

* **Conventional Tail (常规尾翼):** The standard layout with one vertical stabilizer and one horizontal stabilizer positioned lower down on the fuselage.
* **T-Tail (丁字尾 / T型尾):** The horizontal stabilizer is mounted at the very top of the vertical stabilizer, looking like the letter "T".
* **V-Tail (V字尾 / V尾):** A "V"-shaped tail where two slanted surfaces combine the functions of both the rudder and the elevator (commonly used in gliders and some UAVs).
* **Inverted V-Tail (倒V尾):** A V-tail pointing downwards.
* **Twin Tail / Dual Tail (双尾翼):** Features two vertical stabilizers (often seen on larger cargo planes or FPV models to clear the camera view or propeller wash).
