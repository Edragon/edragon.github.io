## Rotorama 008D Pro == 朗视特


Rotorama 008D are basic FPV goggles for starting FPV pilots. The basis is a 4.3" IPS display with an aspect ratio of 16:9 and a resolution of 800x480 pixels, which is sufficient for displaying an analog video signal. The reception is taken care of by a dual receiver supporting all the usual 40 channels. In the package you can find two basic antennas with circular polarization. The goggles support the DVR function, when the received image can be recorded on an SD card of up to 32GB (FAT32). It is possible to connect headphones or an external video receiver.  Power is provided by an integrated battery with a capacity of 2000mAh, which is enough for 2-3 hours of operation. The battery is charged via USB connector. The goggles are delivered in a hard case with a zipper.

- Connector: RP-SMA
- Receiver: Dual 40Ch
- Display: 4.3" IPS 800x480px 16:9
- Battery: Integrated 2000mAh
- Input voltage: 5-23V
- Supporting DVR

## high myopia (typically over -6.00 D) or astigmatism

For pilots with high myopia (typically over -6.00 D) or astigmatism, standard FPV goggles with built-in diopter adjustments (which usually range only from +2.00 D to -6.00 D or -8.00 D) are often insufficient.

Here are the 4 main solutions for correcting vision when using FPV goggles:

---

### 1. Custom Prescription Lens Inserts (Recommended)
This is the most comfortable and reliable solution.
* **How it works**: Vendors produce custom lens frames tailored to your specific FPV goggle model (e.g., DJI Goggles 2/Goggles 3/Integra, FatShark, Walksnail, HDZero). Lenses are custom-cut to your precise prescription, including sphere, cylinder (astigmatism), axis, and interpupillary distance (IPD), and snap directly over the goggle optics.
* **Pros**: Provides precise correction for high myopia and astigmatism, eliminates light leakage and facial pressure, and protects original goggle lenses from scratches.
* **Note**: Provide a full optical prescription (specifically axis and cylinder values if you have astigmatism) when ordering.

### 2. Built-in Diopter + Secondary Corrective Lenses
For goggles with built-in diopter adjustment wheels or sliders (e.g., DJI Goggles 2 / Goggles 3):
* **How it works**: If your vision is -8.00 D and the goggles only adjust down to -6.00 D, set the built-in diopter to maximum (-6.00 D) and attach a secondary -2.00 D corrective lens insert over the eyepiece to combine for a total of -8.00 D correction.

### 3. Wearing Contact Lenses
* **How it works**: Wear contact lenses and set the goggle diopter adjustment back to 0.
* **Pros**: No extra goggle accessories needed; provides a full field of view (FOV). Allows effortless switching between looking through goggles and observing line-of-sight surroundings or the radio transmitter.
* **Cons**: Standard spherical contacts may not fully correct severe astigmatism, and extended use can lead to dry eyes during long flight sessions.

### 4. Using Box Goggles or Thickened Foam Faceplates
* **How it works**: Large single-screen box goggles (e.g., Eachine EV800D) or dual-display goggles equipped with thick faceplate foam spacers can fit over regular prescription eyeglasses.
* **Cons**: Can cause discomfort from eyeglass frames pressing against the temples, potential light leakage, and peripheral image distortion.

---

### Practical Recommendations
1. **Custom lens inserts** are the optimal choice for comfort and image clarity.
2. Ensure your **Interpupillary Distance (IPD)** and **Astigmatism Axis** are measured accurately, as errors in these values at high prescriptions can cause eye strain or dizziness.
